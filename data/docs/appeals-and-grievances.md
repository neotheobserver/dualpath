# Appeals and Grievances

**Document ID:** CH-AG-002
**Effective date:** 1 January 2025
**Owner:** Member Appeals

> Fictional document created for a software demonstration.

---

## 1. Appeal versus grievance

These are different processes with different timelines, and filing the wrong one costs the member time.

An **appeal** contests a coverage decision: a denied claim, a denied authorization, a reduced payment, or a termination of ongoing covered services. Appeals are reviewed on the clinical and contractual merits.

A **grievance** raises dissatisfaction with anything that is not a coverage decision: provider conduct, appointment access, call-centre handling, quality of care, or a billing communication error. Grievances do not change a claim's payment outcome.

If a member submits a grievance about a denied claim, Member Appeals reclassifies it as an appeal and notifies the member in writing. The filing date is preserved.

## 2. Filing deadlines

**Standard deadline: a member has 180 calendar days from the date of the Explanation of Benefits to file a first-level appeal.**

Two exceptions apply:

| Denial code | Filing window | Reason for the exception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DN-TIMELY | **90 calendar days** | Timely-filing denials are contested on documentary evidence that either exists or does not; the shortened window reflects that no clinical review is required. |
| DN-DUPLICATE, DN-CODING | **Not appealable** | These are not coverage decisions. A duplicate denial means the original claim was already adjudicated; a coding denial requires the provider to correct and resubmit. Neither has an outcome for a member to appeal. |

Every other denial code — DN-PRIOR-AUTH, DN-OON, DN-NOT-COVERED, DN-MED-NEC, DN-REFERRAL, DN-ELIGIBILITY and DN-FORMULARY — carries the standard 180-day window.

An appeal received after its deadline is dismissed without review unless the member documents that circumstances beyond their control prevented timely filing.

## 3. Who may file

- The member
- A parent or legal guardian of a minor member
- A personal representative holding a signed Appointment of Representative form
- The **treating provider**, on the member's behalf, for any denial involving medical necessity or prior authorization. Provider-filed appeals do not require a signed appointment form.

## 4. What an appeal must contain

1. Member identifier
2. The claim identifier or authorization reference being appealed
3. The date of service
4. A statement of what the member is asking the plan to do
5. Any clinical records, letters of medical necessity, or documentation supporting the request

For a **medical necessity** appeal (DN-MED-NEC), the plan looks specifically for: the clinical rationale for the chosen treatment, documentation of conservative therapies attempted and their outcomes, and objective findings supporting the request. An appeal consisting only of a statement of disagreement is very unlikely to succeed.

For a **timely filing** appeal (DN-TIMELY), the only evidence that changes the outcome is proof the claim was originally submitted inside the filing window — a clearinghouse acceptance report, an electronic submission acknowledgement, or a plan reference number from the original submission.

For an **out-of-network** appeal (DN-OON), see Section 5 of Network Access and Referrals (CH-NW-003) on network gap exceptions and emergency care.

## 5. Levels of review

### Level 1 — Internal review
Conducted by a reviewer who was not involved in the original decision. Medical necessity appeals are reviewed by a licensed clinician in the same or a similar specialty as the treating provider.

- **Pre-service** requests: decision within 30 calendar days
- **Post-service** claims: decision within 60 calendar days
- **Expedited** requests: decision within 72 hours

### Level 2 — Internal reconsideration
Available if Level 1 upholds the denial. Must be requested within **60 calendar days** of the Level 1 decision letter. Reviewed by a different clinician than Level 1, and decided on the same timelines.

### Level 3 — External review
Conducted by an Independent Review Organisation with no relationship to the plan. Available after Level 2 is exhausted, or immediately if the plan fails to meet its own decision deadline.

- Must be requested within **four months** of the final internal denial
- Available for denials involving medical necessity, appropriateness, level of care, effectiveness, or an experimental/investigational determination
- **Not** available for denials that turn purely on a contractual benefit exclusion, where the service is plainly not a covered benefit
- **The external review decision binds the plan.** If the IRO overturns the denial, the plan pays.

Expedited external review runs in parallel with internal appeals when a delay would seriously jeopardise the member's health or ability to regain maximum function.

## 6. Expedited appeals

An appeal is expedited when the standard timeline could seriously jeopardise the member's life, health, or ability to regain maximum function, or when the member is in the middle of a course of treatment.

Either the member or the treating provider may request expedited handling. **If the treating provider states that the standard timeline would jeopardise the member's health, the plan grants expedited handling without further review of that assertion.**

Expedited decisions are issued within 72 hours and communicated by telephone the same day, followed by written confirmation within three calendar days.

## 7. Continuation of coverage during an appeal

When the plan terminates or reduces a course of ongoing covered treatment, the member may request that coverage continue while the appeal is decided. The request must be made before the termination effective date. If the final decision upholds the termination, the plan may recover the cost of the continued services from the member.

## 8. Outcomes and reprocessing

| Outcome | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Overturned | The claim is reprocessed within 30 calendar days of the decision. Any member payment collected in error is refunded. |
| Partially overturned | The approved portion is reprocessed; the decision letter states which elements remain denied and why. |
| Upheld | The decision letter states the specific plan provision or clinical criterion relied on, and the next available level of review. |

Every decision letter names the specific criterion applied. A letter that says only "not medically necessary" without identifying the criterion is deficient and should be challenged on that basis.

## 9. Where appeals succeed

Internal audit of appeal outcomes shows the strongest predictors of a successful appeal:

1. **DN-PRIOR-AUTH where the service was clinically appropriate.** Usually resolved faster by a retrospective authorization request under CH-UM-004 Section 6 than by an appeal — the 90-day retrospective window is shorter than the 180-day appeal window, so file the retrospective request first and appeal only if it is refused.
2. **DN-REFERRAL where the PCP will confirm the referral existed.** A backdated referral resolves the claim directly.
3. **DN-ELIGIBILITY where the date of service or enrolment record was wrong.** A documentary correction, not a clinical argument.
4. **DN-MED-NEC with complete conservative-therapy documentation.** Succeeds when the record shows what was tried and why it failed.

Appeals of DN-NOT-COVERED denials rarely succeed, because a plain benefit exclusion is contractual rather than clinical — and for the same reason they are generally ineligible for external review.
