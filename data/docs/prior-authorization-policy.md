# Prior Authorization Policy

**Document ID:** CH-UM-004
**Effective date:** 1 July 2025
**Supersedes:** CH-UM-003 (1 January 2024)
**Owner:** Utilization Management

> Fictional document created for a software demonstration.

---

## 1. Purpose

Prior authorization is a coverage review completed **before** a service is delivered. It confirms that the requested service is a covered benefit and meets the plan's clinical criteria. It is not a treatment decision: a denial of authorization does not prevent a member from receiving care, only from having it covered.

A claim submitted for a service on the prior authorization list without an approved authorization on file is denied with code **DN-PRIOR-AUTH**.

## 2. Services requiring prior authorization

Effective 1 July 2025, the following require prior authorization for all plans:

### Advanced imaging
- MRI and MRA, all body regions
- CT and CTA, **except** when ordered from an emergency department
- PET and nuclear cardiology studies
- Any advanced imaging study repeated within 90 days of a prior study of the same body region

### Surgical and procedural
- All non-emergency inpatient admissions
- Arthroscopy, joint replacement and spinal surgery
- Bariatric surgery
- Any procedure performed in an ambulatory surgical centre with an expected allowed amount above $2,500
- Cosmetic or reconstructive procedures of any kind

### Therapies
- Physical, occupational and speech therapy beyond **12 visits** per plan year, per discipline
- Cardiac and pulmonary rehabilitation programmes
- Applied behaviour analysis

### Behavioural health
- Inpatient psychiatric admission
- Residential treatment and partial hospitalisation
- Transcranial magnetic stimulation and electroconvulsive therapy
- Psychological and neuropsychological testing beyond four hours

### Pharmacy and infusion
- All specialty and biologic medications
- Infusion therapy delivered in a facility setting
- Any medication requiring step therapy under the formulary

### Other
- Durable medical equipment with a purchase price above $1,000, and all rentals beyond 60 days
- Home health beyond the first 10 visits
- Skilled nursing facility admission
- Non-emergency medical transport
- Genetic and genomic testing, except the screening panels listed in CH-BN-005

## 3. Services explicitly exempt

Prior authorization is **never** required for:

- Emergency services, including all care delivered in an emergency department
- Post-stabilisation care during an emergency admission, for the first 48 hours
- Preventive services listed in Section 4 of the Member Handbook
- Primary care and specialist office visits
- Standard laboratory and pathology services
- Plain radiography (x-ray) and ultrasound
- The first 12 therapy visits per discipline, per plan year
- Any service delivered while the member is inpatient under an already-approved admission

## 4. How to request authorization

Requests are submitted by the **treating provider**, not the member. A member cannot obtain their own authorization.

The request must include:

1. Member identifier and date of birth
2. Requesting and performing provider identifiers
3. The specific procedure code or codes being requested
4. The primary diagnosis code
5. Clinical documentation supporting medical necessity
6. For therapies: the treatment plan, current functional status and objective progress measures
7. For imaging repeated within 90 days: the reason the prior study was insufficient

Requests are accepted through the provider portal, by secure fax, or by telephone for urgent cases.

## 5. Decision timelines

| Request type | Decision issued within |
| --- | --- |
| Standard (non-urgent) | 14 calendar days of receiving a complete request |
| Expedited / urgent | 72 hours of receiving the request |
| Concurrent review of an active admission | 24 hours |
| Pharmacy — standard | 72 hours |
| Pharmacy — expedited | 24 hours |

If the plan requests additional clinical information, the clock pauses for up to 14 calendar days awaiting the response. If no response arrives within that window, the request is closed as incomplete; a closed request is not a denial and may be resubmitted.

An approved authorization is valid for **90 days** from the date of approval, or for the duration explicitly stated on the approval letter, whichever is longer. Authorizations for a course of therapy state a visit count and an end date; services beyond either limit require a new request.

## 6. Retrospective authorization

An authorization may be requested **after** a service has been delivered only in these circumstances:

- The service was delivered as an emergency and later determined to require authorization
- The member's eligibility was not verifiable at the time of service
- The provider can document a plan system outage at the time of the attempted request
- The service was delivered during an inpatient stay that was itself authorized

Retrospective requests must be received within **90 days of the date of service** and must include the same clinical documentation as a prospective request, plus a written explanation of why authorization was not obtained in advance. Retrospective requests received after 90 days are not reviewed; the denial stands and the provider may not bill the member for the authorization penalty.

**This is the single most common recoverable denial.** A DN-PRIOR-AUTH denial is not final: if the service was clinically appropriate, a retrospective request filed inside the 90-day window is frequently approved and the claim is reprocessed.

## 7. Authorization is not a payment guarantee

An approved authorization confirms medical necessity and benefit coverage as understood at the time of review. Payment still depends on:

- The member being eligible on the date of service
- The benefit not being exhausted (for example, a visit limit already reached)
- The claim being submitted correctly and within the timely filing window
- The service delivered matching the service authorized

A claim may therefore be denied for a different reason despite a valid authorization. Where that happens, the denial code identifies the actual cause; it will not be DN-PRIOR-AUTH.

## 8. Relationship to the July 2025 policy expansion

The 1 July 2025 revision added ambulatory surgical centre procedures above $2,500, repeat advanced imaging within 90 days, and genetic testing to the authorization list. Providers were notified 60 days in advance.

Utilization Management expects an elevated rate of DN-PRIOR-AUTH denials for **dates of service in the third quarter of 2025** while provider workflows adapt to the expanded list. Claims denied in that period for newly added services should be reviewed for retrospective authorization before any appeal is filed, since retrospective approval resolves the claim faster than the appeal process.
