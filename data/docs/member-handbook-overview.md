# Cascade Health Plan — Member Handbook Overview

**Document ID:** CH-MH-001
**Effective date:** 1 January 2025
**Applies to:** All individual and family plans offered in Washington, Oregon and Idaho.

> This document is a fictional artefact created for a software demonstration. Cascade Health Plan does not exist, and nothing here is medical, legal or insurance advice.

---

## 1. How Cascade plans are structured

Cascade offers five plan designs across four metal tiers. Every plan covers the same set of essential health benefits; they differ only in how cost is shared between the plan and the member.

| Plan | Tier | Design intent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cascade Bronze 6500 | Bronze | Lowest premium, highest deductible. Suits members who want catastrophic protection. |
| Cascade Silver 3500 | Silver | Balanced copay-based design. The most commonly enrolled plan. |
| Cascade Silver HSA 4000 | Silver | Health-savings-account compatible. No office copays; all services subject to deductible then coinsurance. |
| Cascade Gold 1500 | Gold | Lower deductible, low copays, higher premium. |
| Cascade Platinum 500 | Platinum | Minimal cost sharing for members with ongoing care needs. |

Exact deductible, copay, coinsurance and out-of-pocket maximum amounts for each plan are held in the plan benefit records and reproduced on your Summary of Benefits and Coverage. **This handbook deliberately does not restate those dollar figures**, because benefit values are updated at each plan year and the benefit record is the single authoritative source.

## 2. The four cost-sharing mechanisms

Members encounter cost sharing in four forms. Understanding which one applies to a given service explains almost every "why did I owe this?" question.

**Deductible.** The amount a member pays for covered services before the plan begins paying. Preventive care listed in Section 4 is exempt: it is covered before the deductible is met.

**Copayment.** A fixed dollar amount for a specific visit type — primary care, specialist, urgent care or emergency room. Copay-based plans (Bronze, Silver 3500, Gold, Platinum) apply copays to office visits without requiring the deductible to be satisfied first.

**Coinsurance.** A percentage of the allowed amount, applied after the deductible has been met. This is the mechanism used for imaging, surgery, facility care and laboratory services on all plans, and for *all* services on the Silver HSA plan.

**Balance billing.** When a member uses an out-of-network provider, the plan pays based on its allowed amount, but the provider has no contract obligating them to accept it. The provider may bill the member for the entire difference between their charge and the plan's allowed amount. This is the single largest driver of unexpectedly high member liability, and it is why network status matters more than any other factor in a member's total cost.

## 3. What "allowed amount" means

The **allowed amount** is the maximum the plan recognises as payable for a service.

- For an **in-network** provider, the allowed amount is set by contract. The provider agrees to write off the difference between their charge and the allowed amount. The member's liability is therefore limited to their deductible, copay or coinsurance.
- For an **out-of-network** provider, the allowed amount is set by the plan's own fee schedule. There is no contractual write-off, so the member is liable for their cost share *plus* the balance.

Because of this, an out-of-network claim and an in-network claim with the same billed charge produce very different member liability, even when the plan's own payment is similar.

## 4. Preventive services covered before the deductible

The following are covered at no member cost share when delivered by an in-network provider:

- One annual preventive or wellness visit per plan year
- Immunisations per the recommended adult and childhood schedules
- Screening colonoscopy at guideline-recommended intervals
- Screening mammography, cervical cancer screening and bone density screening at guideline intervals
- Annual depression and anxiety screening
- Diabetes and cholesterol screening for members meeting the clinical criteria
- Tobacco cessation counselling, up to eight sessions per plan year

A screening service becomes **diagnostic** — and therefore subject to normal cost sharing — when it is performed to investigate an existing symptom or a previously identified abnormality. A colonoscopy ordered because a member reports rectal bleeding is diagnostic, not preventive, even though the procedure is identical.

## 5. Out-of-pocket maximum

Once a member's accumulated deductible, copays and coinsurance for covered in-network services reach the plan's out-of-pocket maximum, the plan pays 100% of covered in-network services for the remainder of the plan year.

The following **do not** count toward the out-of-pocket maximum:

- Premiums
- Balance-billed amounts from out-of-network providers
- Charges for services that are excluded from the plan
- Penalties applied because prior authorisation was not obtained

## 6. Identifying your plan year

All Cascade plans run on a calendar plan year: 1 January to 31 December. Deductibles and out-of-pocket accumulators reset on 1 January. A member who enrols mid-year receives a full deductible for the remainder of that plan year; it is not prorated.
