# Network Access and Referrals

**Document ID:** CH-NW-003
**Effective date:** 1 January 2025
**Owner:** Network Management

> Fictional document created for a software demonstration.

---

## 1. Network tiers

Cascade contracts a single network across Washington, Oregon and Idaho. A provider is either **in-network** (contracted) or **out-of-network** (not contracted). There is no preferred sub-tier.

In-network providers have agreed to a contracted rate and to write off charges above it. Out-of-network providers have agreed to nothing, which has three consequences for the member:

1. The plan pays from its out-of-network fee schedule, which is lower than a contracted rate.
2. The provider may balance-bill the member for the entire difference between their charge and the plan's allowed amount.
3. Balance-billed amounts do **not** count toward the member's out-of-pocket maximum, so they are not capped.

The practical result is that out-of-network care can cost a member several times what the same service costs in network, even where the plan's own payment is comparable. Network status is the single strongest predictor of member liability on a claim.

## 2. Referral requirements

Cascade plans do **not** require a referral for most specialist care. A member may self-refer to any in-network specialist.

Referrals **are** required for:

- Any specialist visit under a plan design that states a referral requirement on the Summary of Benefits
- Out-of-area specialist care arranged through the reciprocity arrangement in Section 7
- Continuing specialist care beyond the initial consultation where the initial visit was authorised as a one-time evaluation

A specialist claim submitted without a required referral is denied **DN-REFERRAL**. This denial may not be billed to the member.

### Referral validity
A referral is valid for **180 days** from its issue date, or for the number of visits it specifies, whichever is exhausted first. Referrals are issued by the member's primary care provider and must be on file with the plan before the specialist claim is submitted — a referral written in the chart but never transmitted to the plan will not prevent a DN-REFERRAL denial.

A **backdated referral** is accepted where the PCP confirms in writing that the referral decision was made before the date of service. This resolves most DN-REFERRAL denials without an appeal.

## 3. Verifying network status

Network status is determined **as of the date of service**, not the date the claim is processed or the date the member checked. A provider who leaves the network between the date a member verified status and the date of service is out of network for that service, unless continuity of care under Section 5 applies.

Members should verify status at the level of the **individual practitioner and the service location**, not the practice name. A contracted practice may employ non-contracted practitioners, and a contracted practitioner may see patients at a non-contracted facility. Facility-based specialties — anaesthesiology, radiology, pathology and emergency medicine — are the usual source of a surprise out-of-network claim, because the member does not choose them.

## 4. Network gap exceptions

A **gap exception** allows an out-of-network provider to be paid at in-network rates, with the member's cost share calculated at the in-network level and balance billing prohibited.

A gap exception is granted when:

- No in-network provider of the required specialty is available within **60 miles** of the member's residence, or
- No in-network provider can offer an appointment within the clinically appropriate timeframe, or **30 calendar days** for routine specialist care, or
- The member requires a sub-specialty or procedure not performed by any in-network provider, or
- The member is mid-treatment and transferring care would be clinically harmful

Requests are submitted by the member or the treating provider **before** the service where possible, and must document the search: which in-network providers were contacted, when, and what they said. A gap exception request with no documented search is denied.

Where the service has already been delivered, a retrospective gap exception may be requested within **90 days of the date of service**, on the same documentary basis. This is the standard route for overturning a **DN-OON** denial.

Approved gap exceptions are valid for the specific provider, service and episode of care named in the approval.

## 5. Continuity of care

When an in-network provider's contract terminates, members in active treatment with that provider may continue at in-network cost sharing for:

- **90 days** from the termination date, for a course of active treatment
- Through the **postpartum period**, for a member in the second or third trimester at the time of termination
- Through the end of life, for a member receiving hospice or terminal care

The member must request continuity of care within 30 days of being notified of the termination. Continuity of care does not extend to new conditions or to care unrelated to the treatment in progress.

## 6. Emergency care

**Emergency services are always covered at the in-network level, regardless of where they are delivered or whether the provider is contracted.**

An emergency is defined by the prudent-layperson standard: a condition manifesting itself by acute symptoms of sufficient severity that a prudent layperson would reasonably expect that failing to obtain immediate care would place their health in serious jeopardy. **The standard is applied to the member's presenting symptoms, not to the final diagnosis.** A member who goes to an emergency department with chest pain that turns out to be reflux has had an emergency for coverage purposes.

For emergency services:

- No prior authorization is required, ever
- The out-of-network claim is priced as though in-network
- Member cost share is calculated at the in-network level
- **Balance billing is prohibited**, and a member who receives a balance bill for emergency services should report it to Member Services for direct resolution with the provider
- Coverage continues through post-stabilisation care for the first 48 hours of an emergency admission

Once the member is stabilised and can safely be transferred, ordinary network and authorization rules resume.

### Ground and air ambulance
Emergency ambulance transport is covered at the in-network level regardless of the transporting service's contract status. Non-emergency medical transport requires prior authorization and is denied without it.

## 7. Out-of-area and travel coverage

Members travelling outside the three-state service area are covered for:

- Emergency and urgent care, at the in-network level
- Follow-up care directly arising from an out-of-area emergency, until the member can reasonably return to the service area

Routine and elective care obtained outside the service area is treated as out-of-network unless a gap exception was approved in advance.

**Students and dependents residing outside the service area** for more than 90 consecutive days may request out-of-area status, which extends in-network cost sharing to routine care from any licensed provider in their location. The request must be made before the coverage period begins.

## 8. Network adequacy standards

Cascade maintains the following appointment-availability standards. A documented failure to meet them is itself grounds for a gap exception:

| Care type | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| Emergency | Immediate, 24/7 |
| Urgent care | Within 48 hours |
| Routine primary care | Within 10 business days |
| Routine specialist care | Within 30 calendar days |
| Behavioural health, non-urgent | Within 10 business days |
| Behavioural health, urgent | Within 48 hours |

Geographic standards: an in-network primary care provider within 30 miles or 30 minutes of the member's residence, and an in-network provider of each contracted specialty within 60 miles.
